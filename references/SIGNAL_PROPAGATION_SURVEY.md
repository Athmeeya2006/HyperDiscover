# Signal-propagation network inference — literature survey

Scoping survey for the scope extension in DECISIONS.md D-030 (perturbation-response inference of edges and
hyperedges). Compiled 2026-10-25 by literature agents (web search; each entry checked against a real page;
`verified: false` = could not confirm). Abstract-level reading unless stated. Not yet merged into references.bib.

## B. Cascade / diffusion traces

Reading B (cascades/diffusion traces) has a mature pairwise literature (NetInf, ConNIe, NetRate, MultiTree, Netrapalli-Sanghavi, Daneshmand) and a thin higher-order one. The only direct higher-order-from-contagion precedent I confirmed is Wang et al. 2022 (binary contagion/Ising data, 2- and 3-body, EM). Landry et al. 2024 does network plus nonparametric contagion function, which is pairwise structure with complex contagion. Tang et al. 2026 (Bayes-THIS) is dynamics-based, not cascade-based, but it is the closest competitor to our identifiability problem. It states that higher-order interactions create lower-order signatures that look like real edges, and that severity depends on cross-order support. This matches our 86-99.6% mimicry finding. My searches found no cascade-timing method that infers hyperedges (joint multi-parent infection) with a real page. Treat that as a gap in what I found, not proof of absence. I did not download any PDFs.

**Gap for us.** 1) Nothing I found infers hyperedges from cascade timing (joint multi-parent infection). The pairwise cascade methods assume independent per-edge hazards. Hyperedge inference would need a hazard of the form lambda_i(t) = sum_j a_ij*1[j infected] + sum_jk b_ijk*1[j and k both infected]. With b = 0 this reduces to NetRate-style pairwise inference. 2) The cascade setting might help identifiability because infection indicators are binary, so the product x_j*x_k is the AND of two indicators. The pairwise-mimicry fraction then depends on how often j and k are infected separately versus together, so the event structure may help. That is a hypothesis; I did not find a paper testing it. 3) Tang et al. 2026 already documents cross-order aliasing in continuous data, so our contribution has to go beyond reporting it. Candidates are the pure-interaction penalty, perturbation tests, and symbolic distillation. 4) Netrapalli-Sanghavi-style per-node decoupling and sample-complexity arguments could give a theory section on how many cascades are needed for hyperedge identifiability.

**Fit with HyperDiscover.** The cascade reading does not fit the current method, which uses continuous states and derivative losses. It would be a separate observation model. The closest S4 analogue is the SIS quenched mean-field system sampled as discrete infection events, with a hazard likelihood replacing the derivative MSE. Our hard-concrete gates and the lambda_t > lambda_p Occam prior carry over directly. Pairwise-only variants and false-positive tests also carry over. The symbolic f, g, h layer would reduce to rate and probability functions of the infected-neighbour count. I suggest treating cascade inversion as a stretch module (Section 10, an optional side study) and not as the core scope. For the main paper, the near-term baseline and positioning items are Bayes-THIS (Tang et al. 2026), Malizia et al. 2024 and Wang et al. 2022, because they target the same higher-order inference problem. Before citing them, run the Phase 4 literature check. Dataset-wise, SIS cascades are cheap to simulate with S4, so this is feasible without new data. Whether it would help closed-hyperedge identifiability remains untested.

## A. Perturbation / pulse response (CHOSEN, D-030)

Verified literature survey. Bottom line: no paper found that infers hyperedges from joint (two-node) perturbations. That is the gap. Perturbation/response network inference is established for pairwise graphs (Timme 2007, Nitzan 2017, Casadiego 2017, Barzel-Barabasi 2013, Hens 2019, Zhang-Witthaut-Timme 2020). The higher-order literature supports the problem statement, but I found no work on the interventional test. Malizia 2024 is observational (checked in its full text). Carletti 2020 notes that responses carry a higher-order imprint. Two 2026 papers (Mouyebe-Bloch; Tang et al. Bayes-THIS) show a linear Jacobian sees hypergraphs only through first-tail-moment 'exposure', and that hyperedges leave spurious lower-order signatures. Both support the claim below. Answer to the key question, from theory and not from any found paper: yes in principle. A single-kick or linear-response (Jacobian) design cannot see hyperedges, because the Jacobian at a fixed point is the sum of pairwise-like terms. A joint kick (j, k, both) gives the mixed second difference d^2 xdot_i/dx_j dx_k, which is exactly the part h contributes that no pairwise term u(x_i,x_j)+u(x_i,x_k) can produce. That is the intervention-based version of the 'pure interaction' quantity. It is also the test CLAUDE.md Stage 3(c) calls for. Caveats: (a) it needs interventional access, which is not available for observational data. (b) It must be done around several operating points, or with strong enough kicks, since a small kick near a fixed point only probes the linear part. (c) Higher-order terms of a pairwise mimic can still produce mixed differences if g is nonlinear AND the pairwise graph has an indirect path j->l->i or k->l->i. So the test needs short-time or direct-response windows, because mediated paths add delay. The mixed second difference at short times is non-zero only for direct triadic coupling. I verified every cited page via search or fetch. I did not read full texts except Malizia (PMC), Bayes-THIS abstract, and the abstracts of the others.

**Gap for us.** I found no paper that uses joint two-node perturbations (kick j, kick k, kick both; non-additive response at i) to identify hyperedges. Perturbation-based reconstruction is pairwise only, and every higher-order reconstruction paper found is observational. The one searched master's thesis (IFISC 2026, microbial time series) is also observational. The gap is therefore open to us, subject to the caveat that the searches were not exhaustive (about 10 queries) and a thorough check for 'interventional hypergraph inference' is still advised. Concrete contribution: define the interventional pure-interaction statistic D_i(j,k) = r_i(j,k) - r_i(j) - r_i(k), where r is the short-time response of node i's rate, or the cumulative response over a short window. Under the model class it equals h's mixed second difference. Pairwise couplings contribute zero to D at first order in time. They contribute only through mediated 2-step paths (j->l->i), which arrive later. Simulate it for S1-S5, including closed hyperedges, and compare AUROC against the observational baseline. This answers CLAUDE.md Stage 3(c) quantitatively and reuses the existing simulators with a kick option. It also gives a causal 'what does an additional kick do' signal that is robust to the 86-99.6% pairwise-mimicking problem, because it removes the pairwise confound by design rather than by regularisation. Also try a 3-way analogue of Barzel-Barabasi silencing on the tensor of non-additive responses to remove indirect contributions, and test the Jacobian-only (single-kick) design as a negative control. Theory predicts it cannot detect hyperedges, per Mouyebe-Bloch.

**Fit with HyperDiscover.** Add an optional interventional data mode. The observation protocol gets a kick schedule: for chosen triples (or a screened subset), simulate trajectories with kick j, kick k, and both, starting from the same state. Screening then uses D_i(j,k) to rank candidate triples. This is cheap because only the K_t screened triples per node need joint kicks, which fits the Stage 1 caps. The structure model gets an extra loss term: predicted non-additivity on held-out kick pairs must match the data. That makes the interventional signal part of training rather than only a held-out check. It extends identifiability experiment (c), which is currently only a held-out test. For the paper, report three regimes: observational only, single-kick (Jacobian) design as the negative control, and joint-kick design. For systems with a small pure-interaction fraction (S4 0.02-0.04, S2 0.07-0.10, S5 0.004), joint kicks should be the main lever. Direct competitors to cite and compare against are Malizia 2024 (MATLAB code available) and THIS and Bayes-THIS (observational). Caveat: interventions are not available for most real data, so the paper must be clear that this is an experimental-design result (when you can perturb, you can identify), alongside the observational method. The 'backtrack the network from signal propagation' request is covered by (i) pairwise graph reconstruction from arrival times and responses, using Hens 2019 and Zhang 2020 as the forward models and Barzel-Barabasi 2013 silencing as the inversion, and (ii) the new joint-kick statistic for hyperedges. I did not find a ready-made inversion of arrival times, so that part is original work. Per CLAUDE.md, ask the human before adding this scope, and log new competitors in DECISIONS.md.

## C. Source localization

Reading C (source localization / backtracking) is a mature field, but almost all of it assumes the network is known and the dynamics are simple (SI/SIR/IC, binary states). Hypergraph source localization is new (2024-2025, four papers found). Joint network-and-source inference exists only for pairwise cascades. Nothing found does continuous-state, symbolic-equation, or hyperedge identification together with source inference. No review was found on arXiv. The only survey found is the Ying & Zhu monograph, which I did not open (UNVERIFIED beyond catalog listings). The Brockmann-Helbing and Ying-Zhu entries rest on press or catalog pages only. Only the entries marked verified=true have a confirmed arXiv or publisher abstract page. No PDFs were downloaded.

**Gap for us.** 1) No source-localization work found uses continuous-state dynamics or a learned or symbolic model. All of it is SI/SIR/IC on a known (hyper)graph. 2) No joint inference of hypergraph, equations and source was found. Joint inference exists only for pairwise cascades (1602.08114, 2010.01400). 3) Source-localization papers treat the topology as given. Ours is the inverse problem, where the source or perturbation is known or controlled and the structure is unknown. 4) The competitor risk is low for the full triple, but the hypergraph SI papers (2507.02523, 2512.00806) cover S4-like dynamics and are natural comparators for the source side. This search was not exhaustive (standard mode, one pass).

**Fit with HyperDiscover.** The useful bridge is to make the signal source an experimental design variable. This is the same idea as the Stage 3(c) perturbation test, and it addresses the identifiability problem. (a) Impulse or localized-seed trajectories give a time-resolved wavefront. Under closed-hyperedge S4, the wavefront arrival time is a function of effective distance, and triadic terms (x_j x_k) only switch on after two neighbours are active. That gives a nonlinear threshold signature that pairwise terms cannot imitate. This matches the finding that sparse initial conditions raised S4-open from 0.04 to 0.27. (b) Backtracking can be defined as a forward-model inversion. Given the fitted hypergraph model, infer the unknown initial seed set and report whether the fitted structure beats a pairwise-only model on source recovery. This is a downstream test for the sec 9.3 out-of-sample suite. (c) Use Pinto-style sparse-observer arrival times and effective-distance geometry as a cheap, model-free screening score for candidate neighbours in Stage 1, and add a hypergraph-aware screening feature (activation only after two active neighbours). (d) Use HDMPN and SSDH as source-side baselines on S4 with a known or inferred hypergraph. Their results show that the quality of the recovered structure translates into localization accuracy. This is a framing suggestion and I have not run any of it. Source-localization theory alone would not fix identifiability, because the observed spread is exactly the data that pairwise models can mimic."
