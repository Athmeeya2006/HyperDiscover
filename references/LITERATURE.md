## 4. Critiques of the closest papers

1. THIS (Delabays 2025). Does: node-wise Taylor expansion up to order 2 in neighbours, SINDy-style thresholded regression, infers pairwise and triadic coefficients; model-free in dynamics (no known f/g/h), tested on Kuramoto-type and others, EEG. Assumptions: coupling is well approximated by low-order polynomial in a sampling box; data near an operating point. Limits for us: fixed polynomial library (no learned functional form, no closed-form output beyond Taylor coefficients); dense library over all triples scales poorly with N; sensitive to noise (hence Bayes-THIS); crucially the paper states that if h decomposes into pairwise functions, "there is no formal difference between a 2-simplex and a closed triangle of links", so reconstruction is ambiguous (PDF text, section on ambiguity), and that higher-order terms leak into lower-order Taylor coefficients (spurious pairwise edges). This is our main baseline (code exists, so reimplementation is unnecessary).
2. Bayes-THIS (Tang 2026). Replaces thresholding by sparse Bayesian ARD regression; adds posterior-predictive check of higher-order signal and interval-based pruning; analyses non-identifiability of lower-order edges and says severity depends on cross-order support (abstract). Still Taylor library; no symbolic or learned h. Overlaps our identifiability discussion; cite and compare.
3. Malizia 2024. Reconstructs full pairwise + higher-order structure "for any dynamics", hypergraph or simplicial, weighted/directed; applications to bacteria and chaotic oscillators. I read only the abstract and availability statement, so assumptions (library, scaling) are not audited here. Closed-hyperedge ambiguity: not examined by me.
4. Li 2024 HoGRC. Reservoir computing with higher-order Granger causality; strong on scalability and forecasting; structure is read out of reservoir weights; no explicit closed-form equations. Not audited beyond abstract/availability.
5. Su 2025. Adaptive LASSO with candidate pairwise (sin) and three-body terms in phase oscillators; explicitly targets distinguishing pairwise vs three-body when dynamics look alike; fixed trigonometric library; small oscillator networks; extends to four-body. Relevant: empirical evidence on pairwise/three-body confusion in the Kuramoto case (our favourable case, 42-63% pure interaction).
6. Hu 2025 / ND2 / Qiu 2024 (symbolic network dynamics). Pretrained or neural-guided symbolic regression of node and edge functions from node trajectories; pairwise adjacency matrix assumed (Hu: Eq. 3 uses pairwise adjacency; higher-order only a preliminary SI attempt; ND2 abstract: reduces network to equivalent 1-D system, 10 benchmark systems). They provide the symbolic-side competition and the evaluation protocol (recovery rate), but do not infer hyperedges. Network is typically given, noise and missing links addressed in Hu.
7. Neuhauser 2024. With hypergraph GIVEN and dynamics type known, derives the minimum effective order of a hypergraph dynamical system; hypergraph NN learns dynamics and order. Not structure discovery; but gives a principled notion of when order-3 terms are dispensable, directly relevant to our low pure-interaction fractions.

## 5. What HyperDiscover adds (honest assessment)

Not pre-empted as far as I could find: no 2025-2026 paper does joint pairwise+triadic structure inference AND closed-form f, g, h distillation. However the claim must be scoped:
- Structure inference of hyperedges from dynamics is NOT new (THIS, Bayes-THIS, Malizia, Li, Su, He). Our structural novelty must rest on flexible learned message functions (non-polynomial, e.g. exp(-lambda|xj-xk|) in S3, rational S5), L0 gates with Occam asymmetry (lambda_t > lambda_p), screening for large N, and rigorous identifiability reporting.
- Symbolic network-dynamics discovery is NOT new (ND2, Hu, Qiu, Gao & Yan); the novelty is extending it to 3-input h with symmetry constraints and with unknown structure.
- Distilling a message function that is only identified up to pairwise-equivalence is a real risk: if the "true" h is 2-14% pure interaction (our measurement for SIS/LV/consensus/mutualistic), then a symbolic h found from data may be mostly an absorbed pairwise part. Our contribution is credible only if we report (i) the sum of couplings, (ii) pure-interaction-only h, (iii) when the split is identifiable.
- Close-in-time risk: THIS-family (Bayes-THIS is active, 2026) and ND2-family groups could add symbolic or learned components; a preprint soon would weaken novelty. Monitor arXiv.

## 6. Identifiability literature

- Delabays 2025 (THIS): explicit statement that linearly decomposable h = h'(xi,xj)+h''(xi,xk)+h'''(xj,xk) makes a hyperedge indistinguishable from a closed triangle of pairwise links; higher-order terms also feed lower-order Taylor coefficients.
- Tang 2026 (Bayes-THIS): shows lower-order spurious signatures from higher-order interactions; severity depends on cross-order support (per abstract and a secondary summary that said open-triad hyperedges inflate pairwise coefficients while closed ones matter less; the latter is from a third-party summary, not verified by me against the paper body).
- Neuhauser 2024: effective order; how much hypergraph structure is needed to reproduce observed dynamics (known dynamics type).
- Su 2025: distinguishing pairwise vs three-body in oscillators is hard because different interactions yield similar dynamical states; adaptive LASSO to separate them.
- Zhang, Lucas, Battiston 2023: hypergraph vs simplicial complex (closed triangles) produce different collective dynamics: relevant to whether closed-hyperedge structure is distinguishable.
- Rosas 2022 and Santoro/Civilini: information-theoretic/synergy notions of high-order; conceptual relevance to "pure interaction".
- Lizotte 2023, Young 2021, Contisciani 2022, Zang 2026: hypergraph reconstruction from static pairwise/noisy network data; they study when group structure can be told from projections but on network data, not ODE dynamics. I did not verify what they say about identifiability.
- Bian 2025: how much is lost projecting hyperedges to pairwise (link prediction).
- Malizia 2024: no identifiability statement confirmed by me.

Implication with our measurement (pure, non-additive share of true triadic variance: 2-14% SIS/LV/consensus/mutualistic; 42-63% Kuramoto): for the first group, an additive pairwise model explains 86-98% of the triadic term's variance, so structure recovery of hyperedges must rely on a small residual and will be fragile under noise; this agrees with the theory that decomposable parts are not identifiable. Kuramoto is the favourable case. Suggests reporting results stratified by pure-interaction fraction.

## 7. Paper ideas / angles

1. Identifiability-first paper: define a "pure-interaction fraction" (functional-ANOVA) for a triadic coupling and show hyperedge-recovery AUROC as a function of it, with theory linking to Delabays's decomposability remark and Bayes-THIS cross-order support. Benchmark plus diagnostic; low-risk, honest.
2. Equivalence-class output: report the minimum-order explanation (Occam gates) plus the invariant sum coupling and the pure part; connect to Neuhauser's effective order. Statistical test for "does this dataset need order 3?" (compare with Bayes-THIS posterior check).
3. Symbolic higher-order message distillation (the original pitch): g and h via PySR from learned messages, beating THIS on non-polynomial couplings (S1, S3, S5) where Taylor libraries fail; compare equation recovery to ND2/Hu with oracle pairwise structure.
4. Intervention design: show perturbation-response data breaks the pairwise/triadic ambiguity (CLAUDE.md experiment (c)); active data collection angle (cf. Ensemble-SINDy active learning).
5. Out-of-sample physics test: predict hysteresis/explosive-sync or epidemic-threshold shifts from the discovered model; show models equivalent on training data can disagree on transitions, thereby quantifying practical cost of non-identifiability.
6. Scaling/benchmark paper: open benchmark (S1-S5, open/closed/mixed, pairwise-only controls for false-positive rate) with THIS, Bayes-THIS, HoGRC, Su-adaptive-LASSO as baselines (all have public code), runtime vs N.

Reading the PDFs changed three statements above.
1. ND2 (Yu, Ding, Li, Nat. Comput. Sci.) explicitly says in its Discussion that extension to higher-order interactions "requires further study", that adapting its NDformer is "nontrivial" (needs LLM-generated higher-order pretraining formulas and hypergraph representation learning), and that ND2 "could synergize with hypergraph inference methods (ref: Delabays et al.) to jointly discover hypergraph structures and higher-order dynamics". Methods section "Systems with higher-order interaction" only sketches generalizing the node-to-edge operators to n-hyperedges. So the authors themselves name our problem as open future work. ND2 can recover structure for pairwise networks with hundreds of nodes by optimizing edge existence, but "scales poorly".
2. Hu 2025 (LLC), SI Section VII "Inferring higher-order network dynamics with multi-node interaction": two worked examples (nonlinear higher-order consensus exp(l|Xk-Xj|)[(Xj-Xi)+(Xk-Xi)], same family as our S3 and Neuhauser 2020; and opinion dynamics -mu Xi + sum A_ijk Xj Xk (1-|Xi|), recovered as -0.298 Xi + sum A_ijk Xj Xk (1.006-|Xi|)), N=100, "by properly extending the signal decoupling module to support multi-node interactions". In both, the 3-hypergraph adjacency tensor A_ijk appears as a given input (observation tuple includes topology A); no hyperedge inference is claimed. So symbolic recovery of higher-order terms with KNOWN hypergraph exists (SI only, two systems, N=100). This does not pre-empt joint structure+equation discovery but it removes any claim that "nobody has distilled symbolic higher-order dynamics".
3. He et al. 2026 (arXiv:2610.31155, posted 3 days before this search): learns "dynamically relevant coupling forms" then sparse regression for directed interactions of several orders in weakly coupled chaotic higher-order networks, exploiting a self-averaging / hub-state reduction. Assumes weak coupling, chaotic nodes, dense hub neighbourhoods; forms are learned as compact functions of the hub state (not closed-form symbolic equations as far as the abstract/intro show; I did not read the method section). It is the closest recent paper to a 'learn coupling form, then reconstruct multi-order structure' pipeline. Does not pre-empt us (regime-specific, library regression at the end, no symbolic distillation shown) but must be cited and, if code appears, compared.

## Closest competitors — full-text check (2026-10-26)

Method: PDFs converted with pdftotext and read (the sections named below, plus keyword sweeps for interven*/perturb*/symbolic/ground-truth/library). New PDFs: papers/wang2025_physics-informed-high-order-graph.pdf, papers/rommens2026_beyond-phenomenology.pdf, papers/calleja-solanas2026_hoi-hidden-ecology.pdf, papers/villegas2026_is-higher-order-physics-different.pdf, papers/timme2007_revealing-connectivity-response.pdf. Correction to the earlier text: Wang 2025 and Rommens 2026 were previously "abstract only"; they are now read in full.

**Wang, Wang, Xue 2025 (arXiv:2510.09082, "PhyHSL").** A forecasting model: a spatio-temporal graph encoder, a low-rank "dynamic hypergraph structure learning" module (incidence matrix Lambda = U W, with hypergraph convolution), then a neural-ODE branch plus a Koopman-operator branch fused by an MLP and trained with an ELBO. Evaluated by MAE on Facebook-pages, web and WS graphs plus three self-built supply-chain networks (700-5000 nodes) against GNN, hypergraph-NN and PINN baselines. The "hypergraph" is a latent, dense, soft embedding used to improve prediction; there is no ground-truth hypergraph, no hyperedge recovery metric, no interpretable hyperedge list, no equations (the "physics-informed" part is a generic neural-ODE/diffusion prior, and interpretability is only asserted), and no intervention data. It does not pre-empt (a) or (b). Cite as forecasting-with-latent-hypergraph related work only.

**Rommens, Traversa, Ferraz de Arruda, Moreno 2026 (arXiv:2609.26031).** A pure theory/position paper with worked toy examples (diffusion, Boolean group dynamics, ecological interactions, K4 clique-projection ambiguity); no inference algorithm, no experiments on data. Contributions: (i) an unrestricted graph with arbitrary node functions can emulate any node-level map (Prop. 1), so fit cannot decide pairwise vs higher-order; (ii) interaction order (number of variables that must act jointly in at least one term) is an invariant of the map, the same in every exact representation (Sec. 3.5); (iii) an MDL criterion, with clique projection costing about (k-1) times the hyperedge list for disjoint groups. Its Sec. 6.4 and 7.1 explicitly state that interventions can discriminate where observation cannot ("Interventions that set (x2,x3,x4)=(1,1,0) distinguish the two dynamics outright"; "interventional data can settle questions that observation alone may not"; a discriminating outcome certifies group sensitivity, a null outcome does not certify aggregate response), and the conclusion recommends adding interventions to empirical studies. This is supportive motivation for (b), conceptually anticipating it for discrete group-membership perturbations, but it proposes no hyperedge-inference method from kicks. For (a): it does not do equation discovery. Note the caveat it implies for us: "order-3 needed" is a statement relative to an admissible model class; our claims must be phrased relative to the class (f + sum g + sum h with shared functions).

**Tang, Srikrishnan, Kulik 2026 (arXiv:2605.04218, Bayes-THIS).** Replaces THIS's STLS by sparse Bayesian regression with ARD over a monomial (Taylor) library around a base point; adds a posterior-predictive check for whether data contain higher-order signal and posterior-interval pruning. Tests on higher-order Kuramoto (phase lag pi/4 to avoid parity cancellation) with n about 15, plus a generalized Lotka-Volterra case; all data are passive trajectories near an equilibrium. Section 6 confirms (now verified in the body, not just a secondary summary) that triadic terms feed pairwise Taylor coefficients, and defines the unsupported-pair fraction q (fraction of triad-pair incidences whose pair has no pairwise edge): q = 0 for a simplicial complex (pairwise false positives then overlap with true edges), large q gives systematic spurious pairwise edges indistinguishable from true ones; it says this applies to any Taylor-based method. It is a pairwise-false-positive result, not a statement that closed hyperedges are unrecoverable. Its Discussion lists "designing good experiments for hypergraph inference from dynamics remains an open problem", a direct gap statement for (b). No symbolic equations (a Taylor library only), no interventions. Does not pre-empt (a) or (b); it is a baseline and a source of the q diagnostic.

**Hu et al. 2025 (Nat. Commun. 16:6226; SI Section VII "Inferring Higher-Order Network Dynamics with Multi-Node Interaction").** The SI states that "by properly extending the signal decoupling module to support multi-nodes interactions" LLC infers two higher-order systems, N = 100, ER topology: nonlinear consensus sum A_ijk exp(l|Xk-Xj|)[(Xj-Xi)+(Xk-Xi)] (inferred form shown with l = 1), and opinion dynamics -mu Xi + sum A_ijk Xj Xk (1-|Xi|) (inferred -0.298 Xi + sum A_ijk Xj Xk (1.006-|Xi|), true mu = 0.3). The SI gives only these two examples and one performance figure. The framework's input is the observed states plus the topology (main text: "given initial states X(0) and topology A", with masks only for pairwise missing-edge studies); the higher-order SI does not describe masking or inferring the 3-hypergraph tensor, and nothing in it is a hyperedge-inference experiment. So: symbolic recovery of higher-order dynamics with the hypergraph given exists (two systems, SI only); joint inference is not shown (I did not find a statement either way about whether A_ijk is hidden in those two runs; treat "given" as likely but UNVERIFIED for those specific runs). No interventions. It narrows the claim "nobody has distilled symbolic higher-order dynamics", it does not pre-empt (a) or (b).

**Wang, Ma, Chen, Lai, Zhang 2022 (Nat. Commun. 13:3043).** Statistical inference plus expectation-maximization reconstructs 2-simplicial complexes (two- and three-body links) from binary time series of two discrete-state processes with known update rules: simplicial SIS-type contagion and simplicial Ising (Glauber-type flips with Hamiltonian J1 sum S_i S_j + J2 sum S_i S_j S_k). Edges are found first and three-body links are then searched among them ("improved two-step strategy"; it notes three-body accuracy depends on two-body inference), so triads are assumed to sit on pairwise edges (simplicial-complex setting, the closed case; open hyperedges are not the target). Real contact data (Hypertext2009, Thiers, InVS, LyonSchool) supply structure; dynamics are simulated. The data are passive; the only "perturbation" is random flipping of states as a robustness test, not a designed intervention. No functional forms are learned or output. It pre-empts neither (a) nor (b). It is a relevant structure-only precedent for discrete dynamics and could be cited next to Malizia 2024 and Delabays 2025.

**Kalabalik et al. 2026 (arXiv:2610.13292).** A Lagrangian NN with prescribed kinetic energy learns the Axilrod-Teller triple-dipole potential for particles (inputs: three distances plus angle cosines) from accelerations, with signal-aware sampling; PySR on the trained potential returns the Axilrod-Teller closed form; embedding in Lennard-Jones shows the three-body term is recovered only when its dynamical signature exceeds residual model error (clean at amplified coupling, not at real noble-gas coupling). It is mechanics for an isolated triple whose existence is known: no network, no choice among candidate triples, no hyperedge inference, no node-level shared message functions, no interventions. It does not pre-empt (a) or (b). Its value to us: a second neural-then-PySR precedent with an explicit observability threshold, supporting our "pure-interaction fraction" framing.

### Extra targeted searches (2019-2026) on interventions / perturbations and higher-order structure (done 2026-10-26, WebSearch only, so recall is limited)

Findings:
- No paper found that infers hyperedges/triadic couplings of a dynamical system from designed single and joint node perturbations. Closest statements are conceptual: Rommens 2026 (above); Villegas and Meloni 2026 (arXiv:2609.20759, perspective: "evidence for a genuinely higher-order mechanism requires ... an intervention rather than an observation, designed so that no pairwise account with the same inputs could follow it"; read the relevant paragraph); Calleja-Solanas et al. 2026 (arXiv:2605.06301: HOI Lotka-Volterra series reproducible by effective pairwise models, "HOIs cannot, in general, be inferred from time-series data alone", fingerprint of HOIs lies in the Hessian of the growth-rate functions rather than the Jacobian, advocates targeted experiments but notes the number of required manipulations grows combinatorially; read in part). Both support our motivation and neither implements an inference method. Calleja-Solanas's combinatorial-cost remark is a point we must address (our kick design is O(N * K^2) per node via screened candidates, not all triples).
- Delabays et al. 2025 (THIS, Nat. Commun. 16:2691), re-read: it says THIS is noninvasive and "does not require perturbing the system in precise ways (e.g., via control input injection)", and that data from a localized region where the dynamics linearize (small noise around a fixed point) cannot reconstruct a hypergraph. Consequence for (b): kicks must be large enough to reach the nonlinear regime (second-order response), otherwise the response at i to kicks of j and k is additive to leading order; the experiment design must control kick amplitude. The follow-up on control (Delabays, Chen, Dorfler, Zhang, De Pasquale, arXiv:2511.08647) uses trajectories near a stable equilibrium plus THIS then controller design: control after inference, not inference by intervention.
- Classical pairwise perturbation-based reconstruction: Timme 2007 (PRL 98:224101, Crossref and arXiv verified; constant driving of phase-oscillator networks, linear equations for coupling matrix; PDF in papers/) and its descendants (the Timme-Casadiego review arXiv:1408.2963 was seen in search results only, UNVERIFIED by me). ### Verdicts on novelty (as of 2026-10-26, subject to the search limits above)

(a) Joint hypergraph structure plus symbolic equation discovery from node time series: NOT pre-empted by any of the six papers read in full. The nearest pieces are separate: structure with fixed or Taylor dynamics (THIS, Bayes-THIS, Malizia 2024, Wang 2022), symbolic recovery with the hypergraph supplied (Hu 2025 SI Sec. VII, two systems, N = 100), and neural-then-PySR for a known single triple (Kalabalik 2026). ND2 itself names the joint problem as future work (see section 8). Residual risk: He et al. 2026 (arXiv:2610.31155, section 8) and any unseen preprints; repeat the three queries before submission. Wording to use: "to our knowledge, first to jointly infer the hypergraph and closed-form coupling functions", never "first symbolic higher-order discovery".

(b) Perturbation/intervention-based hyperedge inference (kick j, kick k, kick both; non-additive response at i implies {i,j,k}): NOT pre-empted. No paper found implements it for dynamical systems. It is, however, conceptually foreshadowed in three 2026 position/theory papers (Rommens et al., Villegas and Meloni, Calleja-Solanas et al.) and has a statistical-biology cousin (response-surface/HDMR/MRA second-order terms, epistasis). So novelty claim should be "a concrete inference method plus identifiability analysis for hyperedges from joint kicks in network ODEs", and the paper should cite these as motivation, not claim the idea of interventions-as-discriminators is new. Two technical cautions found in the literature: (1) linear-regime response cannot identify triadic terms (Delabays 2025), so kick amplitude/regime matters; (2) intervention cost grows combinatorially (Calleja-Solanas), so screening-driven design is a necessary part of the contribution. Also note a limit from Rommens Sec. 7.1: a null result (additive response) does not certify that the mechanism is aggregate/pairwise; a non-additive response certifies joint dependence.
